## Overview {#overview}

Endorphins are the body's own opiates. Their one biological job is to mask physical pain with pleasure so that we keep going when tired or hurt. Sinek groups them with [dopamine]({{< relref "dopamine.md" >}}) as the "selfish" chemicals of progress.


## What they do {#what-they-do}

_([Leaders Eat Last: Why Some Teams Pull Together and Others Don't]({{< relref "../books/leaders_eat_last.md" >}}), Ch. 6)_

-   Evolutionary purpose: persistence hunting. Ancestors who could keep tracking an animal over long distances, and still get home, ate — endorphins made the effort feel good
-   The "runner's high" is this mechanism; regular exercisers can develop a **craving** for it, which is why a run or the gym relaxes after a stressful day
-   Today we mostly get them from exercise or manual labour — and from **laughter**, which releases endorphins to mask the strain on the body
-   Practical use: "you can't laugh and be afraid at the same time" (Stephen Colbert). A little lightheartedness lowers tension in a tense room — a leader's tool, but equally one for a family dinner or a friendship
