## Overview {#overview}

-   Multi-day, self-guided cycling travel along signed long-distance routes, typically
    broken into daily stages (etappen/étapes) that match a rider's fitness and available
    time rather than a fixed race schedule.
-   Distinguished from sport cycling by its emphasis on infrastructure that supports
    ordinary travelers: signposted waymarking, cyclist-friendly accommodation, luggage
    transfer or bike-rental/repair services, and points of interest positioned as
    detours rather than obstacles.
-   A major segment of regional tourism economics in Europe — dedicated portals,
    multilingual sites, and regional cycling associations (e.g. Austria's "Radland
    Kärnten") exist specifically to package a river valley or corridor as a touring
    product.


## Staged itineraries {#staged-itineraries}

-   Long routes are conventionally split into daily "etappen" of roughly 40-80 km,
    each ending in a town with accommodation, so travelers can assemble a multi-day
    trip by combining however many stages fit their schedule.
-   Stage boundaries tend to fall at natural waypoints — a larger town, a border
    crossing, or a change in terrain — rather than at even distance intervals.


## Cyclist-friendly infrastructure {#cyclist-friendly-infrastructure}

-   Specialized rental and repair services cluster along popular routes, letting
    travelers ride one-way or without their own bike.
-   Accommodation providers market themselves specifically to cyclists (secure bike
    storage, drying rooms, early breakfasts, luggage transport between stages).
-   Weather, arrival/route-planning widgets, and trip-bookmarking tools are standard
    features of route websites aimed at trip planning rather than just promotion.
