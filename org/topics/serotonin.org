- so called happiness hormone
- can't pass the blood-brain barrier (that is means you cannot influence it by dietary sources)
  - but tryptophan (building block of serotonin??) can do so
- turns out [[#Kohlenhydrate][carbohydrates]] ingestion shunts non-trytophan amino acids out from the blood and into the muscles (and that allows tryptophan greater access into the brain)

* Pride, status and responsibility
/Source: [[id:e343fcec-6ed1-4e30-91c0-66c6c0a79da8][Leaders Eat Last: Why Some Teams Pull Together and Others Don't]]/
- Serotonin is the feeling of *pride*: what we feel when we believe others like or respect us, or when someone we look after succeeds. It lifts confidence and status (Sinek's example: Sally Field's 1985 Oscar speech)
- It is felt on both sides of a relationship: at a graduation the student *and* the parents in the audience feel it. Its job is to reinforce the bond between parent and child, teacher and student, coach and player, boss and employee
- It makes us feel accountable to *people*, never to numbers: we work hard to make proud those who invested in us, and we feel responsible for those in our care. Crossing a finish line with a cheering crowd (Sinek's New York marathon, with family on the route) feels different from crossing it alone, though the time and effort are identical
- Status is biological and has to be earned: a 2010 study (Gino, Norton, Ariely) found people wearing fake designer clothes felt less pride and were more inclined to cheat elsewhere. Status symbols give only a short boost because no mentor/parent/leader relationship backs them
- Hierarchy as a social contract: we defer to alphas (serotonin raises their status) because we expect them to be the first to protect us — leaders "eat last". See [[id:41898058-fd6b-48c5-bbbd-513625f4e3b2][Leadership]]
