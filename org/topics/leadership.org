#+title: Leadership
#+filetags: :psychology:philosophy:

* Overview
Leadership, in Sinek's account, is not rank or management technique but a *choice to look after the people in your care*, with or without formal authority. It applies wherever people depend on each other — parenting, friendships, a team, a recovery group, a neighbourhood — and it works through the body: the leader creates the conditions in which [[id:2b6e8820-a254-4138-ad80-dc71c97a8082][serotonin]] and [[id:cacce695-0420-4ecb-8ea4-77d8fa31de28][oxytocin]] reward trust and sacrifice instead of [[id:b57f02f1-5f51-4426-a6e4-c3cf721c9cd5][cortisol]] punishing vulnerability.

* Leaders eat last
- Hierarchy exists to prevent fighting over scarce resources: we let the alpha eat first (serotonin raises their status), but in return expect them to run towards danger first. "The cost of leadership is self-interest" (Lt. Gen. George Flynn, USMC)
- This is a social contract, not a numbers question. We resent investment bankers' pay not because of the size but because they kept the perks and sacrificed their people; nobody would object to a bonus for Nelson Mandela
- Authority is not leadership: some people with rank are not leaders, and some at the bottom of an organization are. Perks belong to the *role*, not the person (the ceramic cup versus the Styrofoam cup)
- Trust is "a biological reaction to the belief that someone has our well-being at heart", not agreement on opinions

* Leadership is like parenting
- Bob Chapman (Barry-Wehmiller) realised at a wedding, as a father handed his daughter to her husband, that employers are handed someone's child to care for; the model is a family, where you do not get rid of a child in hard times. In the 2008 downturn the company used furloughs shared by everyone instead of layoffs, and people started to trade time off with each other
- Charlie Kim (Next Jump) took lifetime employment as a rule and treated underperformance as a slump to coach through; revenue growth rose and turnover fell from 40% to 1%
- Sinek: "Everything about being a leader is like being a parent. It is about committing to the well-being of those in our care and having a willingness to make sacrifices to see their interests advanced so that they may carry our banner long after we are gone"
- Failing to feel belonging at work means "we take that struggle home" — the same Circle of Safety is needed in both places. See [[id:b57f02f1-5f51-4426-a6e4-c3cf721c9cd5][Cortisol]] on work-life balance as *where we feel safe*

* Leading in everyday life
- Empathy is "a second by second, minute by minute service" owed to everyone, not something kept for customers (the A-10 pilot who snapped at an exhausted crew chief, then learned the man was up with a new baby)
- Adolescence is where coping strategies are set. Supportive parents, teachers, coaches, family dinners and hobbies teach teenagers to turn to people rather than to alcohol or screens when they feel inadequate — see [[id:b561773c-b424-4366-97ff-a08da298f90f][Dopamine]]
- Recovery needs service: in Alcoholics Anonymous nobody stays sober without Step Twelve, helping another alcoholic. Service to real, knowable people breaks dopamine addiction because it triggers serotonin and oxytocin
- Everyone who got through a setback names someone who stood by them; being that person is leadership
- Leadership is a responsibility to do more, not a licence to do less, and change can be gradual: small acts, experiments, "one day at a time"

* Shared struggle and purpose
- Hardship that is shared bonds people; in abundance there is less reason to cooperate. Leaders need to frame a challenge larger than the resources at hand (a "vision of a world that does not yet exist") so that people have something to commit to
- The "best days" we remember are the shared hardships, not the smooth ones; so a culture of cooperation is also what makes memories worth keeping
- Purpose that serves others, not the self (Milgram: those with a higher purpose resisted harmful orders), outlasts profit or status as a motivator: "Human beings have thrived for fifty thousand years not because we are driven to serve ourselves, but because we are inspired to serve others"

* Caveats
- Sinek's biology is popular-science simplification (the chemicals do not map one-to-one onto feelings), and several claims — the ADHD "addiction" idea, school-shooting and suicide statistics tied to loneliness — are polemical rather than established. Treat the framework as a useful lens, not as settled neuroscience
